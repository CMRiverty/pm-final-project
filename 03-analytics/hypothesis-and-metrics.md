# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** a consumer with multiple open claims, limited time, and variable income who wants to resolve debt digitally.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Successfully set up a repayment plan online that feels affordable, understandable, and manageable without contacting support.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** When selecting a repayment plan, Mara is unsure about the financial consequences, future payments, and whether the chosen plan is the right option, causing hesitation and abandonment.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Contacting customer support, delaying the decision, using calculators or spreadsheets, discussing options with friends/family, or simply postponing repayment.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Consumers who need help the most are abandoning digital repayment plans because they don't understand or trust them, driving costly agent contacts and lower self-service adoption.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** Help hesitant planners confidently choose and complete a repayment plan by making costs, commitments, and options clear and trustworthy, reducing abandonment and support dependency.

## Read the evidence in your case
- **Where does your case say people stall or leave? Does that match the friction you wrote in Module 2?:** The largest drop occurs between the income document step and plan review, where only 77.9% continue. This supports our Module 2 friction hypothesis. Consumers are hesitant to provide income information because they do not understand why it is needed, worry about making mistakes, or experience document upload issues. The data confirms that trust and confidence are critical barriers in the journey.
- **What got better or worse in the case, and does that support the workaround your persona uses?:** Agreement rates improved from 44.2% to 46.0% and self-service resolution increased from 32.3% to 34.7% after the six-month plan was shown first. However, missed-payment rates increased from 15.6% to 19.0%. This adds nuance to our hypothesis. Consumers are completing plans more often, but may be selecting plans they cannot sustainably afford, which supports the workaround behavior of seeking reassurance from agents before committing
- **Which user in the case is carrying the pain, and is that the person your Module 2 persona describes?:** The strongest pain appears among consumers with multiple open claims and variable income patterns. Users with three or more claims have only a 27.6% agreement rate compared to 52.1% for users with a single claim, while agent contact rates rise to 43.0%. Users with variable or shift-based income also perform worse than those with fixed salaries. This strongly aligns with our persona, Mara the Hesitant Planner, who manages multiple claims and uncertain income.

## Step 2 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Consumers hesitate or abandon the journey because they do not understand why income information is requested, fear entering incorrect information, and are uncertain about the consequences of the repayment plan.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** The largest funnel drop occurs between the income document step and plan review, where only 77.9% continue. Additionally, 16.2% of agent contacts are related to "why income is asked," and users with variable income have a lower agreement rate (41.6%) than users with fixed salaries (48.1%).
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Mara the Hesitant Planner, a consumer with multiple open claims and variable income who wants to create an affordable repayment plan digitally but lacks confidence in the information requested and the plan she is selecting
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Consumers do not understand why affordability and income information is required, causing abandonment, support contacts, and reduced completion of digital repayment plans.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Increase successful self-service repayment-plan creation while reducing reliance on agent support.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Increase agreement rate from 46.0% to 50.0%.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Missed-payment rate within 60 days must not increase above the current baseline of 19.0%.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Evaluate after one month of pilot data or after at least 1,000 completed journeys in the target segment.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on interview findings showing that consumers are uncertain about why income information is required, combined with quantitative evidence that the largest funnel drop occurs between the income document step and plan review (77.9% continuation rate) and that 16.2% of agent contacts concern why income is requested, we believe that improving transparency and guidance around affordability and income verification for hesitant planners will increase repayment-plan agreement rates from 46% to 50%, while maintaining the missed-payment rate at or below the current 19% baseline. We will evaluate the outcome after one month of pilot data or 1,000 completed journeys
