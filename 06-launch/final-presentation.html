Slide 5 · Strategy

People drop out where they stop trusting the plan

Problem hook

Consumers who need help the most are abandoning digital repayment plans because they don’t understand or trust them, driving costly agent contacts, failed repayment outcomes, and lost recovery value for Riverty.

Doing nothing means higher servicing costs, flat self-service rates, and a growing competitive gap as competitors digitize debt resolution more effectively.

Value proposition

For consumers who need a repayment plan, we will enable them to understand, trust, and set up a sustainable payment agreement digitally. This creates better outcomes for consumers and lower servicing costs for Riverty.

It is urgent because today’s experience drives avoidable agent contacts, failed repayment plans, and limits growth in Self-Service Rate (SSR).

Hypothesis

Based on interview findings showing that consumers are uncertain about why income information is required, combined with quantitative evidence that the largest funnel drop occurs between the income document step and plan review (77.9% continuation rate) and that 16.2% of agent contacts concern why income is requested, we believe that improving transparency and guidance around affordability and income verification for hesitant planners will increase repayment-plan agreement rates from 46% to 50%, while maintaining the missed-payment rate at or below the current 19% baseline. In the test, the boundary is 20.0%: investigate if the missed-payment rate reaches it.

Slide 8 · Validation

A/B test: does the explainer lift agreement without hurting repayment?

Control (A) vs variant (B)

Control: the current income-entry screen, which asks for income without explaining why it is needed or how it is used.

Variant: the same screen with one change: a short reassurance text at the top, and a full explanation one tap away.

Held constant: app version, eligibility rules, income questions and fields, document upload flow, plan options and their order, reminders and notifications, onboarding, and agent screens.

Primary metricRepayment-plan agreement rate

MDE+4 pts
Sample / arm2,448
Split50 / 50
DurationMinimum 14 days (≥ 2 weekly cycles), or until the sample size per arm is reached
Significancep < 0.05 (95%)
Guardrail60-day missed-payment rate, baseline 19.0%, must not reach 20.0% (+1 pp); investigate if it does
Ship

Agreement improves by at least +4 pp at p < 0.05 and the guardrail holds.

Iterate

Direction is positive but the lift is below +4 pp.

Kill

No improvement, or the primary metric moves negatively.

Slide 9 · Launch

A small, owned launch: the screen itself is the channel

GTM strategy
Goal: Conversion Tier: S (minimal) Owned

Audience: existing consumers in the Collection digital repayment flow who reach the income-entry screen, with the most focus on those with multiple open claims or variable income (like Mara): 27.6% agreement for 3+ claims, 41.6% for variable income.

Why S: a text change inside an existing screen, no new product or pricing. Mostly a release, with light internal enablement so agents give the same explanation as the screen.

Top three channels, all owned
Income-entry screen Phone support and agents Reminder message (email or SMS)

The screen is where the audience hesitates; agents give the same explanation; the reminder adds one line on why income is asked.

Enablement

One-pager with the exact screen wording, short FAQ, updated call script, two-minute demo. Privacy/legal confirm the data-retention wording before launch.

Owners (roles)

Product Manager: plan, A/B test, go/no-go. UX designer: screen. Engineer: build and feature flag. Legal/privacy: data wording. Agent team lead: one-pager and briefing.

Phase 1 · beta

50/50 A/B test for at least 14 days, or until the sample size per arm is reached. Agents briefed before it starts.

Phase 2 · launch moment

If the shipping criteria are met, roll out to 100% and send agents the one-pager and call script.

Phase 3 · post-launch

Monitor agreement rate and the 60-day missed-payment guardrail; review at 30 days. Assumes the test meets its criteria.

Slide 10 · Story

What the data and the prototype taught me

Friction points
  • Only 77.9% continue from the income document step to plan review.
  • 16.2% of agent contacts are about why income is asked.
  • Agreement is 27.6% for users with 3+ claims, against 52.1% for a single claim.
  • Showing the six-month plan first raised agreement (44.2% → 46.0%) but also missed payments (15.6% → 19.0%).
Aha moment

The prototype showed that trust information only lived inside the explainer. Users need reassurance before they open it, so a short reassurance moved onto the income-entry screen.

Key takeaways
  • The guardrail matters as much as the goal. Missed payments became a guardrail with a clear boundary.
  • Sample size changed the plan. Detecting +4 pp from a 46% baseline needs 2,448 per arm, not the 1,000 journeys first assumed.
  • AI executes, the PM decides. The strategy only became useful once I brought the thinking myself.
  • Match the launch to the feature. A feature inside an existing flow calls for conversion and a small, owned launch.

Next: run the test and read it only at the fixed read date, confirm the data-retention wording with legal, and if the criteria are met move on to B3, the affordability check before plan options.
